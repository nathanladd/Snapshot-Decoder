Background

The EGR Flow chart compares two independent ways of measuring how much air is entering the engine. The MAF sensor (ACM_Intake_port_air_flow_maf) measures fresh air entering the intake directly. The speed density model (ACM_Intake_port_air_flow_spd) calculates air flow from intake manifold pressure, temperature, RPM, and volumetric efficiency.

When the EGR valve is closed, both values should track closely together. The difference between them — the error line — should sit near zero. When the EGR valve opens and exhaust gas is recirculated back into the intake, fresh air flowing through the air filter and MAF sensor will be displaced by the exhaust gas. The speed density PID continues to be a calculation of total air entering the engine, while the MAF sensor physically measures fresh air which should now be less than the speed density calculation. A gap between the two lines at that moment is expected and normal.

Point of controversy: The Speed Density Calculation seems to be calculated differently in some ECU software versions. In some cases, it appears to be a calculated Model that will match closely to the MAF sensor reading even when the EGR valve is open and EGR flow is normal. In other cases, it appears to be a more traditional Speed Density Calculation that does not account for EGR flow and therefore will remain above the MAF sensor reading when the EGR valve opens. If your engine is not setting any EGR related faults codes, and your MAF and speed density readings track together closely even when the EGR valve is open, then your ECU is likely using a model that accounts for EGR flow. Don't get too concerned about it - the chart is still useful.

How to Use This Quick Chart

Step 1 — Duplicate the customer's concern while saving a snapshot. Load your snapshot and open the EGR Flow Quick Chart.

The chart plots both air mass flow values on the same axis, with the EGR valve position on a secondary axis. Enable the Time Slider to scrub through operating conditions.

Step 2 — Find a period when the EGR valve is closed (0%).

With the valve closed, the two flow lines should overlap and the error line should be flat near zero. This validates the MAF sensor independent of EGR behavior. Any persistent gap here is not caused by EGR — it points to a MAF sensor issue or an intake air leak downstream of the sensor.

Step 3 — Watch what happens when the EGR valve opens.

As the valve opens, the MAF reading will drop below the speed density model. The gap should grow proportionally with the valve opening percentage. If the EGR valve is open but the gap does not appear — or is smaller than expected for the valve position — either the EGR passage/cooler is restricted or the speed density model accounts for EGR position.

Boost Pressure: Speed density is a calculation partially based on manifold pressure. Low boost pressure caused by a failed turbo or boost leak will cause incorrect speed density readings.

What to Look For

EGR Closed and Open — Normal Operation Normal

With the EGR valve closed, the MAF and speed density readings track together and the error line stays near zero — confirming the MAF sensor is reading accurately with no intake leaks downstream of the sensor. As the EGR valve opens, in this case to almost 47%, MAF (fresh air) drops below the speed density. The difference is about 6.5 grams/second. This is the actual amount of EGR flow. I believe anything less than 10 grams/second would be considered normal.

EGR Open — Restricted Flow Problem

The EGR valve is commanding some opening percentage, but the fresh air measured by the MAF is greater than the calculated engine air flow. It should have gone down to less than the speed density model. The EGR passage is restricted — carbon buildup, a stuck valve, or a blocked cooler is preventing exhaust gas from flowing even though the valve is mechanically opening.

Fault P0400 — EGR Air Plausibility: Set when the ECU calculates that EGR valve flow is below its diagnosis threshold. If the EGR passage is restricted enough that the model and MAF error stays too small during commanded EGR events, this fault will set. Compare the gap at a known valve percentage to a known-good snapshot for the same engine.

Case Study

Case Study
T / S 450 | V1 | 2.4L with EGR Fault Codes

This engine would run for a couple minutes before setting a fault code related to EGR operation. The tech was able to save a snapshot while duplicating the failure. Readings were looking fairly normal until the 'EGR Flow' Quick Chart was opened. The EGR Flow chart below shows an unusually large difference between speed density air flow and actual air flow. In fact, the two readings go in opposite directions which is very strange.

ACM_Intake_Port_Air_Flow_MAF (the orange line) is the actual air flow measured by the MAF sensor.

ACM_Intake_Port_Air_Flow_Spd (the blue line) is a mathmatical calculation by the ECU based on manifold pressure, temperature, and RPM. It is a model of how much air should be entering the engine.

IN_Egr_position (the dashed line) is the position of the EGR valve.

For the first minute of the snapshot, the engine started and idled. During idle the EGR valve was closed and, as expected, the MAF and speed density readings are matching. I placed the cursor on this chart near the point where the engine speed was increased. The EGR valve is still closed at this point. Anytime the EGR valve is closed, we should expect the MAF and speed density readings to be similar, however, in this instance, the air flow dropped.

Engine speed increased, EGR valve remained closed, and yet the air flow not only didn't rise to match the calculated air flow, it dropped. Something's terribly wrong.

It's tempting to dig into the engine looking for air restrictions like a colasping intake hose. Referring to the 'Turbo' Quick Chart will provide additional insight in this case. The Turbo chart shows good engine speed and boost pressure, which rules out intake restrictions. The most likely culprit is a failed MAF sensor that is reading low once engine speed increases. The MAF sensor was replaced. As the second chart shows, the MAF and speed density readings are now tracking together as expected.

New MAF sensor installed — MAF and speed density readings now track together as expected.

Tip: Use the Horizontal Rulers to measure the difference in flow values. A difference of around 10 Grams/Second when the EGR valve is open indicates an expected amount of EGR flow.
Related chart: The EGR Calibration Quick Chart evaluates the mechanical health of the EGR valve itself — whether its actual position matches the learned closed position and whether it is responding to commands. If the EGR Flow chart shows restricted flow but the valve appears to be opening correctly in the EGR Calibration chart, the restriction is in the passage or cooler, not the valve actuator.
