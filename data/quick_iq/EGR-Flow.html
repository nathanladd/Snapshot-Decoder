Background

The EGR Flow chart compares two independent ways of measuring how much air is entering the engine. The MAF sensor (ACM_Intake_port_air_flow_maf) measures fresh air entering the intake directly. The speed density model (ACM_Intake_port_air_flow_spd) calculates expected fresh air from intake manifold pressure, temperature, RPM, and volumetric efficiency.

When the EGR valve is closed, both values should track closely together. The difference between them — the error line — should sit near zero. When the EGR valve opens and exhaust gas is recirculated back into the intake, the speed density model accounts for the additional charge pressure while the MAF sensor only measures fresh air. A gap between the two lines at that moment is expected and normal.

Key concept: The MAF sensor sits upstream of the EGR mixing point. When the EGR valve opens, recirculated exhaust enters the intake after the MAF sensor and is not measured by it. The speed density model, which is based on manifold pressure, does see the combined charge. So when EGR is flowing: model > MAF is expected — the gap represents EGR flow contribution.

How to Use This Quick Chart

Step 1 — Load your snapshot and open the EGR Flow Quick Chart.

The chart plots both air mass flow values on the same axis, with the EGR valve position on a secondary axis. Enable the Time Slider to scrub through operating conditions.

Step 2 — Find a period when the EGR valve is closed (0%).

With the valve closed, the two flow lines should overlap and the error line should be flat near zero. This validates the MAF sensor independent of EGR behavior. Any persistent gap here is not caused by EGR — it points to a MAF sensor issue or an intake air leak downstream of the sensor.

Step 3 — Watch what happens when the EGR valve opens.

As the valve opens, the speed density model will rise above the MAF reading. The gap should grow proportionally with the valve opening percentage. If the EGR valve is open but the gap does not appear — or is smaller than expected for the valve position — the EGR passage is restricted.

What to Look For

EGR Closed — MAF Validated Normal

Both flow lines track together and the error line stays near zero. This confirms the MAF sensor is reading accurately and there are no intake leaks between the sensor and the intake manifold. The EGR system is not affecting the reading.

EGR Open — Healthy Flow Gap Normal

When the EGR valve opens, the speed density model rises above the MAF reading. The gap reflects the volume of exhaust gas being recirculated. A gap proportional to the valve opening confirms the EGR system is flowing as commanded.

EGR Open — Restricted Flow Problem

The EGR valve is commanding some opening percentage, but the fresh air measured by the MAF is greater than the calculated engine air flow. It should have gone down to less than the speed density model. The EGR passage is restricted — carbon buildup, a stuck valve, or a blocked cooler is preventing exhaust gas from flowing even though the valve is mechanically opening.

Fault P0400 — EGR Air Plausibility: Set when the ECU calculates that EGR valve flow is below its diagnosis threshold. If the EGR passage is restricted enough that the model and MAF error stays too small during commanded EGR events, this fault will set. Compare the gap at a known valve percentage to a known-good snapshot for the same engine.
Large Gap with EGR Closed Problem

The speed density model reads significantly higher than the MAF sensor even with the EGR valve fully closed. Since EGR is not flowing, this gap is not an EGR problem — it indicates that air is entering the intake downstream of the MAF sensor without being measured, or the MAF sensor is reading low.

  • Intake air leak — a loose boot, cracked intercooler hose, or failed connection after the MAF allows unmetered air into the manifold
  • MAF sensor reading low — contaminated, damaged, or failed MAF sensor; a new MAF will close the gap
  • Aftertreatment restriction — a blocked DOC or DPF can alter pressure dynamics and cause apparent MAF/model divergence

Case Study

Case Study
T / S 450 | V1 | 2.4L with EGR Fault Codes

This engine would run for a couple minutes before setting a fault code related to EGR operation. The tech was able to save a snapshot while duplicating the failure. Readings were looking fairly normal until the 'EGR Flow' Quick Chart was opened. The EGR Flow chart below shows an unusually large difference between speed density air flow and actual air flow. In fact, the two readings go in opposite directions which is very strange.

ACM_Intake_Port_Air_Flow_MAF (the orange line) is the actual air flow measured by the MAF sensor.

ACM_Intake_Port_Air_Flow_Spd (the blue line) is a mathmatical calculation by the ECU based on manifold pressure, temperature, and RPM. It is a model of how much air should be entering the engine.

IN_Egr_position (the dashed line) is the position of the EGR valve.

For the first minute of the snapshot, the engine started and idled. During idle the EGR valve was closed and, as expected, the MAF and speed density readings are matching. I placed the cursor on this chart near the point where the engine speed was increased. The EGR valve is still closed at this point. Anytime the EGR valve is closed, we should expect the MAF and speed density readings to be similar, however, in this instance, the air flow dropped.

Engine speed increased, EGR valve remained closed, and yet the air flow not only didn't rise to match the calculated air flow, it dropped. Something's terribly wrong.

It's tempting to dig into the engine looking for air restrictions like a colasping intake hose. Referring to the 'Turbo' Quick Chart will provice additional insight in this case. The Turbo chart shows good engine speed and boost pressure, which rules out intake restrictions. The most likely culprit is a failed MAF sensor that is reading low once engine speed increases. A new MAF sensor would be expected to bring the orange line up and closer to the blue line, especially during the period when the EGR valve is closed.

P0404 EGR Position Control Fault Position error (demand vs. IN_Egr_position) exceeds 5% for more than 20 seconds — valve mechanically stuck
Tip: Use the Horizontal Rulers to measure the difference in flow values. A difference of around 10 Grams/Second when the EGR valve is open indicates an expected amount of EGR flow.
Related chart: The EGR Calibration Quick Chart evaluates the mechanical health of the EGR valve itself — whether its actual position matches the learned closed position and whether it is responding to commands. If the EGR Flow chart shows restricted flow but the valve appears to be opening correctly in the EGR Calibration chart, the restriction is in the passage or cooler, not the valve actuator.
